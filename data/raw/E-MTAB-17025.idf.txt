Investigation Title	A vegan diet epigenetically modulates inflammatory pathways and biological aging: Genome-wide DNA methylation analysis of a one-month isocaloric vegan versus meat-rich dietary intervention
Experiment Description	We investigated the impact of a vegan and meat-rich diet on the underlying epigenetic modfications of DNA methylation signatures in the blood of human study participants. Therefore, we analyzed genome-wide DNA methylation from two human cohorts at baseline and following an one-month dietary intervention. The resulting data enabled us to evaluate the impact of both dietary strategies on epigenetic regulation and compare them to each other. We aimed to identify diet-associated shifts relevant to biological processes and potential effects on health outcomes.

Protocol Description	Blood from each participant was collected and aliquoted in six separate 1.5-mL cryovials and stored at −20°C until measurement or transportation to external laboratories.	DNA was extracted from whole blood using a salting-out procedure as described by Miller et al. (1998). Erythrocytes were lysed using a hypotonic lysis buffer and leukocytes were isolated by centrifugation. Nuclei of leukocytes were subsequently lysed using SDS and Proteinase K to release genomic DNA. DNA was isolated by increasing the salt concentration and subsequent centrifugation. The resulting supernatant containing DNA was precipitated using isopropanol.	Cryovials for serum were labeled without information about group. External laboratories did not receive information concerning patient randomization. A numeric code was used for anonymization. Samples could only be linked to a participant using a master list available only to the head of the study.	DNA methylation was determined from DNA samples using the Illumina Human Methylation Epic Beadchip and was carried out by the Human Genomics Facility of the Genetic Department of Internal Medicine at Erasmus MC, Rotterdam, NL.	Readout of the Illumina EPIC array was performed by the Human Genomics Facility of the Genetic Department of Internal Medicine at Erasmus MC, Rotterdam, NL.  Feature extraction from “.idat” files was done in R (version 4.2.3) using the “minfi” package (version 1.44.0) and the corresponding Illumina Manifest files from the R-libraries “IlluminaHumanMethylationEPICmanifest” & “IlluminaHumanMethylationEPICanno.ilm10b4.hg19”.  Poor quality samples were assessed using minfi’s detection p-value function, but all 96 samples passed the threshold of 0.05. Subsequently, poor quality probes, probes associated with sex chromosomes, and probes associated with common SNPs were removed. For all remaining files beta- and M-values were calculated.  	Methylation data was preprocessed into a MethylSet object and normalized using either functional normalization preprocessFunnorm() with NOOB background correction and dye normalization enabled or preprocessQuantile()
